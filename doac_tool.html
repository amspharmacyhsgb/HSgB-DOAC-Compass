🆘 Management of Bleeding & DOAC Reversal 9. DOAC CME Slides9 6. DOAC Newsletter (Mar 2025)6
Bleeding Severity Classification (based on ISTH definition9. DOAC CME Slides9)
Minor Bleeding
  • Bruising, minor cuts
  • Mild epistaxis or gum bleeding
  • Minor haematuria (no clots)
Action: Delay or skip next dose. Apply local pressure. No reversal agent needed. Discuss with prescriber.
Major / Clinically Relevant Bleeding
  • GI bleed requiring transfusion
  • Significant haematuria with clots
  • Haemoptysis
  • Haemoglobin drop ≥20 g/L
Action: Withhold DOAC. Supportive care & resuscitation. Discuss with specialist urgently.
Life-Threatening Bleeding
  • Intracranial haemorrhage
  • Haemodynamic instability
  • Critical organ bleeding
  • Transfusion ≥2 units packed red cells
Action: Immediate reversal agent. Specialist involvement mandatory. Consider ICU.
General Measures (all severities)
  1. Stop DOAC immediately
  2. Record last dose time and dose taken — guides management window
  3. Apply local pressure / haemostasis where applicable
  4. Supportive care — IV access, fluid resuscitation, transfusion if indicated
  5. Activated charcoal if recent ingestion and airway is protected:
    • Dabigatran: within 2–4 hours of ingestion
    • Rivaroxaban / Apixaban / Edoxaban: within 1–2 hours of ingestion
  6. Discuss with specialist — haematology, physician or relevant surgical team
Reversal Agents (severe / life-threatening bleeding or emergency surgery)
🔷 Dabigatran (Pradaxa®)
1st LINE Idarucizumab (Praxbind®)
  • IV 5g STAT — administered as two separate 2.5g doses ≤15 min apart
  • Specific antidote — rapid, complete reversal
IF UNAVAILABLE IV Octaplex (4-factor PCC)
  • 50 units/kg STAT (max 4,000 units)
  • Non-specific; less evidence than Idarucizumab
CONSIDER Haemodialysis
If dabigatran level very high, especially with renal impairment
🟢 Apixaban · 🔴 Rivaroxaban · 🟣 Edoxaban
⚠️
Andexanet alfa — specific FXa inhibitor antidote — NOT available at HSgB
ALTERNATIVE IV Octaplex (4-factor PCC)
  • 25–50 units/kg STAT
  • or 2,000 units STAT (fixed dose)
  • Non-specific reversal strategy — use in life-threatening bleeding only
⚠️ Cessation of DOAC alone may be sufficient in minor bleeding given the short half-life of DOACs. Reversal agents are reserved for severe or life-threatening haemorrhage and emergency procedures. 📋 Dabigatran (Pradaxa®) — Direct Thrombin Inhibitor — Twice Daily
🎯 What is this medicine for?
This medicine helps prevent blood clots from forming. It is used to:
  • Prevent stroke in patients with atrial fibrillation (irregular heartbeat)
  • Treat and prevent blood clots in veins (DVT/PE)
💊 How to take
Dose & frequency: As prescribed (110mg or 150mg), taken TWICE daily at the same times each day.
IMPORTANT: Swallow capsule WHOLE. Do NOT break, chew, bite, or open the capsule. This can increase bleeding risk.
Can be taken with or without food.
For DVT/PE treatment: You will have injections (Clexane/Heparin) for at least 5 days first, then switch to Dabigatran. Do not stop injections until told by your doctor.
⏰ Missed dose
If you miss a dose — take it as soon as you remember, as long as there are more than 6 hours before the next scheduled dose.
If less than 6 hours: skip the missed dose. Do not double the dose.
🩸 Signs of bleeding — Seek medical attention IMMEDIATELY
Contact doctor/go to A&E if you experience:
  • Unusual or prolonged bleeding from cuts
  • Blood in urine (pink/red/dark urine)
  • Blood in stools (black tarry stools, or visible blood)
  • Coughing or vomiting blood
  • Unusual bruising
  • Severe headache, dizziness, weakness (may suggest internal bleeding)
💊 Drug Interactions
Inform ALL healthcare providers (including dentists, surgeons) that you are on this medication.
AVOID: Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Carbamazepine (reduces effectiveness).
Avoid taking NSAIDs (e.g. Ibuprofen, Naproxen) or aspirin unless specifically prescribed — increases bleeding risk.
Inform doctor about: Verapamil or Amiodarone (may require dose reduction).
🗄️ Storage
Keep capsules in original container. Dabigatran capsules are moisture-sensitive — do not store in pill organizers or remove from original packaging.
📋 Other Important Points
Carry a DOAC medication card at all times.
Limit alcohol consumption.
Do NOT stop taking this medicine without consulting your doctor.
No routine blood tests (INR) are required — unlike warfarin.
Reversal agent: Idarucizumab (Praxbind®) is available at this hospital for emergencies.
📋 Apixaban (Eliquis®) — Factor Xa Inhibitor — Twice Daily
🎯 What is this medicine for?
This medicine prevents blood clots. Used for stroke prevention in AF, and treatment/prevention of DVT/PE.
💊 How to take
Dose & frequency: As prescribed, taken TWICE daily.
Can be taken with or without food.
For DVT/PE: Higher lead-in dose first — 10mg BD × 7 days, then reduce to 5mg BD.
⚠️ Very important to switch to maintenance dose on Day 8 — continuing lead-in dose risks serious bleeding.
Tablets can be crushed and mixed in water if unable to swallow whole.
⏰ Missed dose
Take the missed dose on the same day as soon as you remember. Do not take two doses on the same day.
🩸 Signs of bleeding
Seek immediate medical attention for: unusual bleeding, blood in urine/stools, coughing blood, severe headache, unusual bruising, prolonged bleeding from cuts.
💊 Drug Interactions
AVOID: Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Carbamazepine.
Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin unless prescribed. Limit alcohol.
Inform all healthcare providers including dentist and surgeon.
📋 Other Important Points
Carry DOAC medication card. Do NOT stop without doctor's advice. No routine INR monitoring needed.
📋 Rivaroxaban (Xarelto®) — Factor Xa Inhibitor — Once Daily
🎯 What is this medicine for?
Prevents stroke in AF, and treats/prevents blood clots (DVT/PE).
💊 How to take
MUST be taken WITH FOOD (for 15mg and 20mg doses). Taking without food significantly reduces absorption.
Dose & frequency: As prescribed, taken ONCE daily.
For DVT/PE: Lead-in dose — 15mg BD × 21 days (with food), then reduce to 20mg OD.
⚠️ Very important to switch to maintenance dose after Day 21.
Tablets can be crushed and mixed in water with food if unable to swallow whole.
⏰ Missed dose
Take the missed dose on the same day as soon as you remember. Do not take two doses on the same day.
🩸 Signs of bleeding
Seek immediate medical attention for: unusual bleeding, blood in urine/stools, coughing blood, severe headache, unusual bruising, prolonged bleeding from cuts.
💊 Drug Interactions
AVOID: Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Carbamazepine.
Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin unless prescribed. Limit alcohol.
Inform all healthcare providers. Carry DOAC medication card.
📋 Edoxaban (Lixiana®) — Factor Xa Inhibitor — Once Daily
🎯 What is this medicine for?
Prevents stroke in AF, and treats/prevents blood clots (DVT/PE).
💊 How to take
Dose & frequency: As prescribed, taken ONCE daily.
Can be taken with or without food.
For DVT/PE: You will have injections (Clexane/Heparin) for at least 5 days first, then switch to Edoxaban.
Tablets can be crushed and mixed in a small amount of water (stable up to 4 hours).
⏰ Missed dose
Take the missed dose on the same day as soon as you remember. Do not take two doses on the same day.
🩸 Signs of bleeding
Seek immediate medical attention for: unusual bleeding, blood in urine/stools, coughing blood, severe headache, unusual bruising, prolonged bleeding from cuts.
💊 Drug Interactions
AVOID: Phenytoin, Rifampicin, Carbamazepine.
Dose may be reduced to 30mg OD with: Cyclosporin, Dronedarone, Erythromycin, Ketoconazole — confirm with prescriber.
Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin unless prescribed. Limit alcohol.
Inform all healthcare providers. Carry DOAC medication card.
⚠️ Special Considerations
Not recommended for SPAF if CrCl >95 ml/min (increased ischemic stroke risk).

❤️ Understanding DAPT & Triple Therapy13. DAPT Duration Guide (Jan 2025)13
Definitions
DAPT (Dual Antiplatelet Therapy)
Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor (Clopidogrel, Ticagrelor, or Prasugrel)
Used after ACS or PCI/stenting
Triple Therapy
OAC (DOAC or Warfarin) + Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor
AF patient who undergoes PCI/ACS — requires both anticoagulation AND DAPT
Dual Antithrombotic Therapy (DAT)
OAC + P2Y12 inhibitor (Clopidogrel) — without Aspirin
Transition strategy to reduce bleeding while maintaining stent protection
Key Risk Tools
CHA₂DS₂-VASc — Stroke risk in AF. Score ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women) = anticoagulation recommended.
HAS-BLED — Bleeding risk. Score ≥3 = high risk. Does NOT preclude OAC — reassess modifiable factors.
PRECISE-DAPT / PARIS — Bleeding risk on DAPT (ischaemic/haemorrhagic risk scoring for DAPT duration guidance).

💊 DOAC Choice in AF + PCI Context6. DOAC Newsletter (Mar 2025)613. DAPT Duration Guide (Jan 2025)13
Based on major trials (PIONEER AF-PCI, RE-DUAL PCI, AUGUSTUS, ENTRUST-AF PCI) — DOACs are preferred over Warfarin in AF+PCI due to lower bleeding risk.
DOAC Trial Regimen studied Key finding
Rivaroxaban PIONEER AF-PCI Riva 15mg OD + P2Y12 (1yr)
or Riva 2.5mg BD + DAPT		 Lower bleeding vs triple therapy with warfarin. Riva 15mg OD + P2Y12 preferred.
Dabigatran RE-DUAL PCI Dabi 110mg BD + P2Y12
or Dabi 150mg BD + P2Y12		 Lower bleeding vs warfarin triple therapy. 110mg BD used in elderly/renal impairment.
Apixaban AUGUSTUS Apix 5mg BD + P2Y12 ± Aspirin Apixaban + P2Y12 without aspirin: lowest bleeding. Aspirin added ↑ bleeding without ischaemic benefit.
Edoxaban ENTRUST-AF PCI Edox 60mg OD + P2Y12 Non-inferior to warfarin triple therapy for bleeding; similar ischaemic outcomes.
Preferred P2Y12: Clopidogrel is the preferred P2Y12 inhibitor in triple/dual antithrombotic therapy (lower bleeding risk vs Ticagrelor or Prasugrel). Avoid Prasugrel in triple therapy.

🩸 Minimising Bleeding Risk in Triple / Dual Therapy13. DAPT Duration Guide (Jan 2025)13
Pharmacist Checklist
Suggested PPI in Antithrombotic Therapy
All patients on triple therapy should receive a PPI.
High-risk patients on DAPT (Hx GI bleed, age >65, H. pylori, steroids) should also receive a PPI.
PPI options: Pantoprazole 40mg OD, Omeprazole 20mg OD, or Esomeprazole 20mg OD.
Note: Some evidence suggests Omeprazole/Esomeprazole may modestly reduce Clopidogrel efficacy (CYP2C19 interaction) — Pantoprazole or Lansoprazole preferred with Clopidogrel.
Common Prescribing Errors to Flag
Wrong Aspirin dose — prescribing 150mg or 300mg instead of 75–100mg OD.
Continuing triple therapy beyond planned duration without review.
Using Prasugrel in AF patient on OAC.
No PPI prescribed for patient on triple therapy.
